## The Problem

Statistical Justice emerged from work in Colombia with transgender communities, and was developed as part of the work of Liga de Salud Trans. It grew from a simple but urgent problem: many trans people are not properly recognized in the official systems meant to count them.

In official records, people may be registered under the wrong name, the wrong gender, or in ways that fail to reflect who they were. This is not just a technical mistake. It is a deeper failure of recognition and governance. When official data does not reflect people as they are, inequality becomes harder to trace, public action harder to shape, and institutional harm easier to overlook.

While the framework was born from this work in Colombia, it was designed to travel. Statistical Justice offers a way to think across contexts, populations, and data systems about what happens when recognition fails — and what it would take for official data to more fully reflect the diversity of societies.
